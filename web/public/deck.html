The model

Get paid for data
that's actually new.

An agent bonds a stake and submits a record. A scorer measures how much new information it adds, and mints royalty shares in proportion. Buyers pay for access, and 70% flows to shareholders — on every sale, forever.

The reward is a claim on real revenue, not points. That one difference is what farming can't beat.
bond a stake→scored on novelty→mint royalty shares→earn on every sale
Why it holds

Four ways to cheat.
Four ways it fails.

Verbatim copy
Rejected by the contract at the door — the transaction reverts, no bond taken.
Padded copy
Containment catches it after the padding defeats similarity. That's exactly why the check exists.
Reworded record
Similarity flags it at 0.95 against the original. Bond forfeited.
Off-topic filler
Zero of the curator's domain terms. Out of scope, bond forfeited.
Minting is never free — 20% of every bond pays the holders it dilutes. Junk earns a claim on nothing while costing real money.
Business model

The protocol earns on
every sale, forever.

10% of every access sale is the protocol's cut — and mint fees plus slashed bonds accrue to holders, so quality funds itself. Data lives off-chain; only settlement is on-chain, so the marginal cost of a corpus is close to zero.
The flywheel: more contributors → richer datasets → more buyers → more revenue → more contributors.
Why on-chain · why Monad

Per-record accounting,
at agent speed.

Agents owned by different people need neutral settlement — nobody trusts one company's points database. Provenance is a product a buyer can audit. And royalty shares trade, so datasets finally get a price.

Paying agents per record needs 10,000 TPS and sub-cent fees. It dies on Ethereum L1. Monad is the only place the loop feels live — and isolated corpora execute in parallel.

What we didn't solve

The honest edges.

We'd rather say these out loud than have a judge find them.

01
The scorer is a trusted oracle. It can't touch funds, but it decides what mints. Staked or optimistic scoring is next.
02
Character-level similarity misses deep paraphrase. Model embeddings close it — the gate is built for the swap.
03
Slash exclusion is per-address — a second wallet gets around it. That's an identity problem, and a test proves it rather than hiding it.
04
No revocation yet for an accepted record that later proves wrong. A challenge market is the answer.

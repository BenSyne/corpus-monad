Corpus.

Provenance-backed data royalties for the agent economy. Agents get paid for contributing data that is genuinely new — and the rewards are claims on real revenue, not points.

Monad Blitz Toronto · 25 Jul 2026 Built & verified 55 contract tests 2 adversarial review rounds e2e green twice consecutively
Problem

AI labs pay eight figures for licensed data, but only large platforms can sell. Agents are becoming the best collectors on earth with no way to get paid — and every naive "points for data" scheme dies to farming.

Fix

Bond, submit, get scored on novelty, mint royalty shares. Buyers pay for access; 70% flows to contributors forever. Junk forfeits its bond.

Why it holds

Minting is never free — 20% of each bond pays the holders it dilutes. Rewards are claims on revenue, so farming junk earns claims on nothing while costing money.

What the adversarial reviews changed

Two independent review rounds ran before any code was written. They did not rubber-stamp the design — they broke it. Three findings were fatal.

Round 1 — the dominant strategy was garbage

Scoring pure novelty meant off-topic text scored higher than real data (unrelated prose is maximally distant from everything in the corpus). An attacker could mint ~93% of all royalties for the cost of gas. Fixed with a corpus-scope gate and a relevance multiplier on the payout.

Round 1 — padding defeats similarity

Cosine on normalized vectors is length-sensitive: append roughly a quarter more text to a verbatim copy and it slips under any sane threshold. Plagiarism-plus-filler beat honest work. Fixed with a length-invariant containment check. Our demo's padded attack scores 0.847 against a 0.85 threshold — it genuinely evades similarity, which is exactly why containment exists.

Round 2 — the second run would have died on stage

A stale scorer cursor pointing past a fresh chain's head means nothing ever gets scored; a stale accepted-set means honest records get slashed as duplicates of themselves. The run that matters is always the second one. Fixed with self-resetting state, a reset script, and an acceptance criterion of two consecutive clean runs.

Also fixed: pendingOf was never decremented (would have bricked every agent after 5 submissions), three unspecified division-by-zero paths, a curator/scorer honeypot, hash squatting of expired work, perpetual-access economics, and an unbounded pause. All 27 findings are recorded in notes_and_research.md.

The seven gates

1Schemaevery declared field present and non-empty
2Hash integritystored bytes must match the on-chain commitment
3Lengthtrivially short content can't claim a novelty bonus
4Coherencegibberish is maximally "novel" — this is what stops it
5Corpus scopemust use the curator's declared domain vocabulary
6Similarityis this substantially the same information? (≥ 0.85)
7Containmentbackstop for copies hidden inside padding (≥ 0.80)

Relevance was originally specified as cosine distance to a topic vector. Measured against real seed data, that could not separate topics at all — honest records scored 0.43 and an off-topic logistics record scored 0.44. Character trigrams are excellent at catching near-duplicates and useless at judging subject matter. The curator's declared lexicon separates cleanly: honest records match 4–11 domain terms, the off-topic record matches zero.

The invariant that matters: the contract must always hold enough to pay everything it owes.

balance ≥ Σ credits + Σ withdrawable dividends + Σ pending bonds

Fuzzed over random call sequences rather than random amounts — bond and price are fixed at construction, so fuzzing values would only explore unreachable states. Dividend truncation always rounds in the contract's favour, so the remainder is stranded dust, never a deficit.

Money flow

flowchart TD BOND[Bond 0.1 MON] --> D{Scorer verdict} D -->|accepted| MF[20% mint fee to existing holders] D -->|accepted| RB[80% returned to contributor] D -->|rejected| SL[100% forfeited to holders] D -->|scorer silent| RC[95% reclaimed after timeout] MF --> POOL[Holder dividend pool] SL --> POOL BUY[Access purchase 1 MON] -->|70%| POOL BUY -->|20%| CUR[Curator] BUY -->|10%| PRO[Protocol] POOL -->|pull payment| H[Contributors]

Ordering is load-bearing: the mint fee is distributed before the new shares exist, so a new contributor never collects a slice of the fee they just paid. When there is nobody to pay — an empty corpus, or a rejected contributor who holds every share — the money is refunded rather than divided, so no path can divide by zero.

Why on-chain, and why Monad

Why a blockchain

Agents owned by different parties need neutral settlement — nobody trusts one company's points database. Per-record provenance is a product feature: a lab buying training data can audit exactly where every row came from. And royalty shares are transferable, so datasets get a price.

Why Monad

Per-record micro-accounting needs 10,000 TPS and sub-cent fees; this dies on Ethereum L1. 600ms finality is why the loop feels live on stage. Each corpus has isolated state, so submissions to different corpora have disjoint write sets — the parallelism is across corpora, not within one.

What we did not solve

Stated up front, not discovered by a judge
  • The scorer is a trusted oracle. It cannot touch funds, but it decides what gets minted. Contributors are protected if it goes offline (bond reclaim after timeout), not if it turns hostile. Staked or optimistic scoring with a challenge window is the path.
  • Deep semantic paraphrase evades character-level similarity. That needs model embeddings; the gate interface is built for the swap.
  • Slash exclusion is per-address, so a second wallet recovers part of its own slash. There is a test that proves this rather than hiding it.
  • No revocation of an accepted record that later proves wrong. A challenge market is the answer.
  • Front-running a submission on a public mempool needs commit-reveal binding the hash to the sender.
  • Wash-trading leaks 10%, not 30% — the curator cut is recoverable by the curator, and zero via a self-deployed factory.
